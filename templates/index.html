How to Read This

A plain-language guide to interpreting Isolation Forest scores and parameters.

What the anomaly score means

Isolation Forest builds random decision trees that split your data. Unusual points sit far from dense clusters and take fewer splits to isolate. Scores are normalized from 0.00 (very typical) to 1.00 (most isolated).

How Contamination sets the threshold

Contamination tells the model roughly what fraction of rows you expect to be outliers. Raising it lowers the score threshold so more borderline points are flagged; choosing auto lets the algorithm pick its default boundary.

Stability vs. speed with n_estimators

n_estimators controls how many random trees are averaged together. Using 150–300 trees produces more repeatable results across runs, while 20–50 trees gives faster feedback on larger CSVs.

Candidates for review, not confirmed problems

Flagged points are statistical outliers relative to the chosen columns—not automatic proof of fraud, attack, or defect. Always inspect high-scoring rows with domain context before taking action.
